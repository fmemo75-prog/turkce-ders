Noun Compounds

How Turkish links two nouns to express possession and attribution — with 10 audio examples.

Type 1 — Belirtili Tamlama

[Noun + -(n)ın/in/un/ün] + [Noun + -(s)ı/i/u/ü]

The possessor takes a genitive suffix; the possessed takes a possessive suffix. Ownership is definite — "the dog's bone," not just "a dog bone."

Last vowelGenitivePoss. 3sg
a / ı-ın / -nın-ı / -sı
e / i-in / -nin-i / -si
o / u-un / -nun-u / -su
ö / ü-ün / -nün-ü / -sü

Type 2 — Belirtisiz Tamlama

[Noun (bare)] + [Noun + -(s)ı/i/u/ü]

The first noun has no suffix — it acts as a classifier or category. Like English compounds: "apple tea," "bus stop." The second noun still takes the possessive suffix.


Quick test: Can you say "the X of Y"? → Type 1 (definite).
Is it more like "X-type Y"? → Type 2 (indefinite compound).
